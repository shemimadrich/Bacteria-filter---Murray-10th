Specimen Collection, Transport & CSF Quick-Reference

1. Blood Cultures & Sepsis (Ch. 78, Table 78.2)

Skin Prep: 70% alcohol followed by 2% chlorhexidine-alcohol (or povidone-iodine); allow to dry to prevent CoNS/C. acnes contamination.

Volume & Timing: Most septic patients have <1 organism/mL (exceptions: overwhelming S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, B. anthracis). Draw 20 mL/set (2 bottles) for adults; collect 2–4 sets per 24 h.

Mycobacteria / Fungi: 8–10 mL in lysis-centrifugation or dedicated mycobacterial media.

2. Respiratory Specimens (Ch. 79–80, Table 80.2)

Expectorated Sputum: Deep cough into sterile cup (1–2 mL routine; ≥10 mL for mycobacteria/fungi). NEVER refrigerate/freeze (cold kills S. pneumoniae) and NEVER use expectorated sputum for anaerobes (oral flora contamination).

Anaerobic Lung Culture: Requires a protected specimen brush / bronchoscopy or direct pleural aspirate placed into an anaerobic transport tube.

3. CSF Meningitis Patterns (Ch. 81)

  • Bacterial Meningitis: High WBC (neutrophil predominance), elevated protein, low glucose (Gram stain, culture, multiplex PCR).
  • Fungal / TB Meningitis: Mononuclear/lymphocytic cells, mildly-to-markedly elevated protein, low glucose.
  • Viral (Aseptic) Meningitis: Lymphocytic predominance, elevated protein, normal glucose.
  • Subdural Empyema / Brain Abscess: Diagnose by CT/MRI—avoid lumbar puncture (risk of cerebral herniation!).

4. Urine & Genital Specimens (Ch. 82, Table 82.3)

UTI (Cystitis / Pyelonephritis): Midstream clean-catch (discard first void to avoid urethral flora); transport immediately, refrigerate, or use preservative so colony counts don't falsely rise.

Urethritis (N. gonorrhoeae, C. trachomatis, M. genitalium): Use FIRST-VOID urine (do NOT discard first portion!) or urethral/endocervical swab for NAAT.

Genital Ulcers: Syphilis → bedside darkfield microscopy or serology; H. ducreyi → notify lab for slow culture; K. granulomatis → microscopy (unculturable).

5. Gastrointestinal Specimens (Ch. 83, Table 83.2)

Stool Culture & Multiplex PCR: Collect liquid/bloody stool in a clean dry container and transport at room temperature within 1 hour—or transfer to Cary-Blair holding medium (stable up to 72 h). Avoid rectal swabs except in infants/children. Note: NAAT and stool antigen tests cannot be run on O&P preservative vials. Abdominal/Peritoneal/Bile Fluids: Percutaneous aspirate into a sterile anaerobic transport system.
